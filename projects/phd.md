<h1> Hallucination, Omission, Emotion: The explanation of parliamentary text by large language models </h1>

<p> My PhD follows on from my masters thesis project, which assessed the readability of federal parlimentary texts in Australia. </p>

<p><blockquote> <strong> TLDR for my masters thesis: </strong>
Legislation is complex and despite there being a document type to help explain it to the public (the 'explanatory memorandum'), these documents are still written at a very high readability level. The explanatory memorandum is often less helpful than it appears. The thesis examines explanatory memoranda in terms of readability, jargon, common vocabulary, and design structures. </blockquote></p>

<p>Within my PhD, I'm looking at a much smaller selection of legislative texts, as I'm sending them to three large language models and asking them to produce explainer documents at a lower 'readability' level based on four key prompts. This means I end up with a <em>lot</em> of text to analyse. </p>

<p>I'm planning on producing a few articles from this work, which I'll drop below once they've been formally published. </p>
